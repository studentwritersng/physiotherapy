The brief formula + the loop
The pipeline is only as good as the brief you feed it. Three lines, then one sentence that turns the
whole line into a loop.
LINE 1 — WHO IT’S FOR
One sentence, one audience. "Freelance bookkeepers who hate their current tool." A page for
everyone converges to the average — which is exactly the slop you’re escaping.
LINE 2 — HOW IT SHOULD FEEL (USE RECIPE NAMES)
Name it from page 3: "Linear precision", "Aesop calm", "Bloomberg Terminal density". A named
recipe carries a palette, a typeface, a spacing system, and signature moves — "modern and clean"
carries nothing.
LINE 3 — ONE THING TO AVOID
One explicit ban: "no centered-hero SaaS template", "no gradient mesh". A single named anti-
goal steers harder than five vague goals.
THE LOOP — ADD THIS SENTENCE, VERBATIM
"Screenshot your work and iterate until it matches the brief."
With webapp-testing installed, this one sentence gives Claude eyes: Playwright opens the site, screenshots it,
Claude critiques its own render, and fixes it — design, build, look, fix — until it matches. Without it, every skill in
the line is a suggestion.
THE TWO-SCHOOL MOTION RULE
Emil for the product, Meng To for the story. Product UI gets restraint — interface animations under
300 ms, never default easing. The marketing scroll gets cinema — GSAP, Lenis smooth scroll,
pinned sections. Buttons and menus are not a film; your story section is not a form.
NEVER BY DEFAULT — THE 3 BANNED AI LOOKS
Anthropic’s frontend-design skill bans these outright, hex codes included: 1) cream + serif + terracotta (the
#F4F1EA look) · 2) near-black + acid-green "techno" · 3) broadsheet hairline editorial. Each is fine when
chosen
— banned as a
default
, because defaults are how every AI page ends up identical.
